NEPA took the light on the third night of the harmattan, and nobody in the compound was surprised. Mama Nkem lit the kerosene lamp and set it on the table between us like a peace offering.

I read the fifth letter by its yellow glow. He had written it in Kaduna during a storm, he said, and the ink had run where the roof leaked onto his desk.

Some of the words were gone. The ones that stayed were enough.

Why I wrote the letters first

Before there was a story there were twelve letters in a notebook my late uncle gave me. I wrote one every harmattan for twelve years. When I finally wrote the novel, the letters were already there, waiting for someone to find them in a biscuit tin.
